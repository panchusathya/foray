# What actually makes this application undeniable

## First, the premise

*"Everyone's coming from Harvard and Stanford going after robotics and defense."*

**Consensus is not an advantage.** If a reviewer reads forty defense applications in a week, the
forty-first is invisible. You are the only recruiting application in that stack, and that is an
asset — **conditional on one thing: your reasoning has to beat theirs.**

That's the real asymmetry. A consensus market lets you skip the argument; everyone already agrees
defense matters, so the applicant only has to prove capability. A non-consensus market makes you
*earn* the market itself. Harder — and the only route to being remembered, because the reviewer
has to actually think to evaluate you, and thinking is what makes something stick.

So the failure mode isn't "recruiting is boring." It's **"you didn't show me you know something."**

The Harvard/Stanford robotics applicant is betting on **technical ability inside a consensus

## What actually survives 500 applications

Six things. Everything else is noise:

1. **A number nobody else could have.** Not "8 customers" — a number that implies structural
   effort. *"Twelve of twelve referrals converted to interviews."*
2. **A sentence that reframes the market.** *"LinkedIn is a profile graph, not an intent graph"*
   is that sentence. It makes the reviewer stop.
3. **Evidence of taste** — you rejected the obvious thing, on the record, for a stated reason.
   Everyone builds the AI sourcing tool; you looked at it and concluded sourcing is the
   commoditised half.
4. **Proof you ship abnormally fast.** 259 commits in five days, while both of you held full-time
   jobs.
5. **Unfair access** a stranger cannot replicate.
6. **One detail only you could possibly have written.**

# The questions

None of these fill a field. Each is designed to surface something a Harvard/Stanford robotics
applicant structurally cannot produce. **Answer them badly and fast — the raw version is what I
need.**

## A. Taste — your only truly unique credential

1. **Of the 21 companies you sourced, which one did you fight for internally and lose?** What did
   you see that the committee didn't? *(Conviction under disagreement is a far stronger signal
   than a hit rate. Everyone shows their winners.)*
2. **Which company did you pass on that you now think you got wrong — and why did you get it
   wrong?**
3. **You see ~1,000 companies a year. What's the pattern you've noticed that isn't in anyone's
   memo yet?**
4. 🔴 **You have professionally evaluated a thousand alternatives. Why is this the one you'd spend
   your life on?** This is the single most powerful question available to you and nobody else
   applying can answer it with authority. Take it seriously.

## B. Proprietary insight — what you know that isn't public

5. **What did the 15–20 recruiters tell you that contradicted what you expected going in?** The
   surprise is the signal; agreement is worthless.
6. **What have you learned from the ~20 candidates you've moved that isn't in any blog post?**
7. **What do you believe about hiring that most people in the industry would argue with?**
8. **Even at tiny n — what has the reply-classification data already shown you that surprised
   you?** Response times, ghost rates, anything. A real observation from your own instrument beats
   a market-size claim.

## C. The one that would win the application on its own

9. 🔴 **What's a moment where a candidate told you something they would never have put on
   LinkedIn?** A comp floor. A boss they're escaping. "I'd leave tomorrow but only for a founding
   role." **If you have even one of these, it is the most valuable sentence in the entire pack** —
   your whole thesis, proven in miniature, with a real human, in a way no competitor can
   manufacture. Get it anonymised and get it in.
10. **What did they say next?** What did you do with it? Did it lead anywhere?

## D. Speed and intensity

11. **What's the fastest you've gone from idea to production on this?** Name the thing and the
    hours.
12. **What shipped in the last seven days?** Specifically.
13. **What did you build that you then deleted?** Killing your own work reads better than shipping
    volume.

## E. Unfair access

14. **Who can you get on the phone within an hour that a stranger couldn't?**
15. **Which of your eight companies would take a reference call right now — and what would they
    say, in their words?** *(Then actually ask one. A quotable line from a real customer is worth
    more than a paragraph of yours.)*
16. **What could you do for one of a16z's or Greylock's portfolio companies this week that no
    other applicant could?** *(See `networking.md` — this is also the best cold open you have.)*

## F. Conviction under cost

17. 🔴 **What have you actually given up so far?** You both hold seats people in your cohort want.
    Naming the cost is the only thing that makes "we're serious" credible.
18. **What number by December makes leaving obvious — and what happens if you miss it?** A founder
    with a stated kill condition reads as far more serious than one with only an upside case.

## G. The detail only you could have written

19. **What's the strangest or most specific thing that has happened since you started?** The
    inbox getting blocked. A candidate calling the voice agent at 2am. Anything true and odd.
20. **Has someone said no to you in a way that taught you something?**

## H. The uncomfortable two — every strong reviewer respects these

21. **What's the strongest argument against your company, and what's your answer?** Steelman
    yourself before they do. It's the fastest way to read as senior.
22. **If this fails, what's the most likely reason?** Answer it in one sentence, honestly. Most
    applicants dodge this and every reviewer notices.
