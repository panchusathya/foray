# Positioning — infra vs. firm

Brainstorm, then a recommendation. Feeds A1, A2, A5, A6 and the first line of every form.

---

## The tension, stated plainly

**Why you want infra:** services businesses trade at 2–3× revenue and infra at 15–30×.
"Recruiting firm" is a crowded, unglamorous category. a16z speedrun and Greylock are wired to
respond to infrastructure. All true.

**Why it's dangerous right now:** you have **$0 revenue, no one paying for a product, and your
only real traction is services delivery** — ~20 candidates, one interview in process. Claim infra
with services traction and no bridge between them, and a reader spots the gap in one paragraph.
That specific mismatch is one of the most common reasons a strong application gets passed on.

**The graveyard is also worth respecting.** Nearly every dead recruiting-tech company positioned
as infrastructure. And your own research argues against it — Kay Kakish, Candidate Labs:
*"the moat is recruiters and brand, not AI."*

So the question isn't "infra or firm." It's **which infra claim is actually true today, and what
bridges it to what you can prove.**

---

## Five candidate positions

### 1. The firm *(current)*
> An AI-native recruiting firm. We own the outcome; the software is why one recruiter does the
> work of ten.

**For:** matches traction exactly. Clear buyer, known price, no category education needed. The
Crosby comparison does real work.
**Against:** services multiple. Reads as an agency with good tooling to anyone scanning quickly.

### 2. The system of record for hiring outcomes
> Every ATS records process. None of them record outcomes. We're building the outcome layer for
> hiring.

**For:** this is your genuine insight, and it's uncopyable — the origin position plus the return
channel is a seat only one company can occupy.
**Against:** it's a *future* product. You have almost no outcome data yet, and "system of record"
is a category that takes years and a lot of capital to win.

### 3. The execution layer — rails for hiring actions
> Apply, schedule, chase, verify — the actions hiring runs on, as primitives, executed by agents.

**For:** infra-shaped, and you've built the hard parts: browser agent, voice, text, unified CRM.
**Against:** **who buys it?** ATSs won't (you'd be a feature). Agencies famously won't pay for
seats. This has the worst GTM answer of the five and you have no evidence for any of them.

### 4. The mid-funnel agent
> Sourcing is solved and commoditised. The middle — qualify, keep warm, chase, schedule — has no
> product. We built it.

**For:** exactly what you built, and six of eight recruiters told you it's the bottleneck. Clear
buyer.
**Against:** it's a product, not infrastructure, and an ATS could ship it as a feature.

### 5. The verification and outcome layer *(the one that's underexplored)*
> AI collapsed the signal on both sides of hiring. We're rebuilding it.

**For:** this is *timely, true, and structurally infra*. You wrote it yourself in the SPC
answer — LinkedIn is full of AI slop, candidates fire applications into the void. Both sides now
use AI, so response rates go to zero and **nobody can tell who is real or what converts.** In that
market the scarce assets are exactly the two things you're positioned to own: **verified humans**
(intake through a real conversation rather than a parsed resume) and **outcome data** (measurable
only from the origin).
**Against:** nascent. Nobody is writing cheques for "verified human" yet.

---

## The recommendation: infra thesis, firm engine

Don't choose. **Layer it.** Lead with the thesis, prove with the firm, and put one bridge sentence
between them so the services read as strategy rather than compromise.

**The one-liner:**

> Hiring's signal has collapsed — both sides now use AI, so nobody can tell who's real or what
> actually converts. We're building the verification and outcome layer for hiring: every candidate
> verified through a real conversation, every application sent from our own rails, every employer
> reply classified and joined back to the candidate and the role.

**The bridge — this sentence is load-bearing, don't cut it:**

> We run the firm ourselves because it's the only way to sit in the flow and collect that. An ATS
> sees one company. A job board hands off at the click. A sourcing tool stops at the intro. The
> origin of the application is the one seat where cross-company hiring outcomes can be measured,
> and you can't rent it — you have to do the work.

That does four things at once: it's an infra claim, it's honest, it explains why an agency is the
correct wedge rather than a fallback, and it answers *why now* (AI broke the signal) and *why you*
(you occupy the seat).

**Per form:**
- **a16z, Greylock** — lead infra. Both reward thesis, and Greylock's whole form is about whether
  you arrived at the problem honestly, which this framing answers directly.
- **HF0** — lead with what's shipped. It's the only pure-company form and it wants concreteness,
  not category creation.
- **Soma** — split: thesis in the "what are you working on," shipped reality in the progress half.

## Language swaps that do the work

Positioning lives in word choice more than in argument. Same facts, different frame:

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "an AI-native recruiting firm" | "the verification and outcome layer for hiring" |
| "we place candidates" | "we sit at the origin of the application and hold the return channel" |
| "our AI recruiter" | "the agent that runs the middle of the funnel" |
| "we run an agency" | "we operate the firm as the first customer of our own infrastructure" |
| "candidate database" | "200K-profile index with an offline eval harness — nDCG@10, MRR, P@k" |
| "we screen candidates" | "every candidate is verified through a real conversation, not a parsed resume" |

**But do not scrub the firm out.** A reader who can't find the business model gets suspicious, and
"we own the outcome" is a genuine strength — it's what separates you from every tool vendor in the
category. The firm should be *visible and deliberate*, not hidden.

---

## Two costs of going infra-forward, priced honestly

**1. It raises the bar on the technical founder question.** An infra pitch invites "who's building
this." Tushar's YC form says *not a technical founder*; Sathya has to be the visible centre of
gravity — Berkeley CS, first four-wheel odometry system in FIRST, a simulator used by 10,000
teams, the sourcing tooling an $8B fund runs on. **The eval harness, the compliance design and the
reply-classification schema become the proof points**, not the candidate counts.

**2. It raises the bar on "who pays."** The firm answers this and infra currently doesn't. Which
is precisely why the bridge sentence stays: the firm isn't the fallback, it's the acquisition
strategy for the thing that becomes infrastructure.

**The trigger for actually becoming infra**, worth naming so it isn't just narrative: when the
outcome data is dense enough to price risk — to underwrite a longer guarantee at a lower fee than
any agency can match, or to publish per-company response latency nobody else can measure. That's
a 2027 milestone. Until then infra is the thesis and the firm is the business, and saying so
plainly reads as clarity rather than hedging.
