## Z1 - What is the project you are currently working on or would like to pursue? Why? (limit: 500)

Foray — the intent graph for hiring. Nobody knows who's actually in the market, so matching fails
and everyone drowns it in volume. Intent is private, conditional and perishable — no profile page
can hold it. We capture what would move someone, privately, and match on it. We run the searches
ourselves, so demand exists day one: two clients signed this week at 20% of base, ~$232K contingent
across four roles, one founder offered to angel in. We evaluate markets for a living; we picked
this one.

## Z2 - What problem are you solving? (limit: 500)

On LinkedIn you can't express interest without leaking it — your boss, your reports and three
recruiters you never wanted to hear from all find out. So the people worth $30,000 a placement stay
silent, and the industry fights over the small, visible slice willing to be seen looking. We
interviewed about 25 recruiters. They didn't tell us sourcing was hard. They told us they are all
reaching the same candidates as everyone else and none has proprietary distribution. Hiring isn't
slow. It's blind.

## Z3 - What expertise do you have to execute on the work that you want to do? (limit: 500)

Sourcing at volume is our day job — we're both investors at an $8B fund, ~1,000 companies spoken to
in a year and two to three times that outreached. Tushar sourced 21 companies: seven acquired,
fourteen raised from a16z, Sequoia and Khosla. He led campus and lateral recruiting at J.P. Morgan

## Z4 - Who are your competitors and what do you understand about your idea that they don't? (limit: 500)

Sourcing tools — Juicebox, Gem, SeekOut — sell the commoditised half; an index is worth the same to
the first buyer and the thousandth. Agencies have real moats in recruiters and brand, which is
why they can't scale past headcount. Mass-apply consumer products are loud, and the people
worth placing are quiet. What we understand: every one of them competes on discovering public data.
Proprietary supply is the one thing none of them has, and intent can't be scraped, because it is
never published.

## Z91 - Tell us about a risk you've taken or a challenge you've faced. — Tushar (limit: 500)

In college I founded an autonomous drone delivery company. Sole founder: I did the CAD, the
Raspberry Pi work, taught myself aerodynamics from a book, wrote the grants, and recruited master's
students onto the team. We won the ABC Innovation Prize, made Blackstone Launchpad finals, raised

## Z92 - Tell us about a risk you've taken or a challenge you've faced. — Sathya (limit: 500)

At sixteen I made our robotics team's funding problem mine, and started cold-calling manufacturers
for parts. I built the largest prospecting list on the team — twenty companies — and sixteen said
no. Four didn't: Misumi cut 30% off our linear slides, Curbell and Interstate donated the Delrin it
was machined from. Most people who write about robotics write about the robot. We didn't get to
build that robot unless someone convinced strangers to pay for it. I stopped minding being told no.
