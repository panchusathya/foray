## B12 — Anything else we should know

*(a16z and Greylock use identical wording. One answer, used twice. This is the right place to
put A6 — the competitive insight — because neither form has a box for it.)*

Three things.

**One — the thing we know that the category doesn't.** Every product in AI recruiting is sold
against sourcing, because sourcing demos well. We interviewed ~15 recruiters and recorded eight,
and six named the middle of the funnel — qualifying, keeping warm, chasing, scheduling, reading
the soft signals that mean a candidate is about to take another offer — as where they actually
lose money. Not one of their vendors has built for it. That is what we built first, and it is
why we run the firm rather than sell the tool: the middle is only automatable if you own the
whole relationship.

**Two — the position we're accumulating.** Every application we send carries its own inbound
alias, and every employer reply is classified — acknowledged, rejected, interview, assessment —
with a timestamp, joined to candidate and req. An ATS sees one company; a job board hands off at
the click; a sourcing tool stops at the intro. Being the origin of the application *and* holding
the return channel is the only seat from which hiring outcomes can be measured across companies.
That data gets better with every application we send and is worth nothing to whoever starts
tomorrow. It is also, eventually, what lets us underwrite a longer guarantee at a lower fee than
any agency can match.

**Three — what we're missing, since you'll find it anyway.** We have no revenue: everything
delivered to eight companies so far has been free, deliberately, to earn the right to go back
with a price — that conversation is happening this week. Our cold email response rate is 2–3%
and our inbox was once flagged for spam; the recruiters we interviewed say LinkedIn beats it
10–15×, and we believe them. We are incorporating now. We would rather you hear those from us.
